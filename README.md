# Chicago, 1932 (working title)

A top-down, hand-painted gangster sandbox set in Prohibition-era Chicago, built in Godot 4.7 (GL Compatibility). It grew out of a copy of the HyperCars racing game, whose driving physics it keeps; everything racing-specific has been retired. Design: `docs/chicago_1932_game_design_document.md`. Decisions and the current objective: `docs/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`.

You play a nighttime henchman: the whole game takes place at night. **Milestone 003 (A Night's Work):** the Riverside Ward is a small working criminal neighbourhood. You wake in your flat at 7:30 pm, buy bootleg rye from Sal the barber, sell it at the Copper Owl for more, lean on the bartender and the grocer for protection money, buy .45 rounds from Eddie in the back of the hardware store, and get home to bed before dawn. Walk into the shops (their roofs open up), talk to the people, watch the clock; a few cars and passers-by are out late, a squad car waits in an alley, and the beat cop still comes running when you shoot. The cars now drive like heavy 1930s road cars, and you can see their front wheels steer. **Milestone 004 (Street Smarts):** the cars show at a glance which way they face, a painted badge floats over the door of every place you can walk into (brighter as you come near, the flat in its own house-shaped badge), E goes in and out by any door, and a small map in the bottom-left corner shows the streets round you, those places and the beat cop. **Milestone 005 (The City Has Ears):** you are Danny "Dimes" Mercer, and the protection money you collect belongs to **Rourke**, who sits at his corner table in the Copper Owl and expects his cut. The ward lives on its own: the shops and the club have tills, stock, customers and bills, trade through the night and the day while you sleep, and run short when their tills run dry; prices follow what is on the shelves. Rourke knows only what he sees and hears, people remember how you treated them and talk to each other, and a shopkeeper short of money for his delivery is your problem or your opportunity. A new game opens with Danny's own voice over the ward at night, the Copper Owl's singer is heard through its walls and in full inside, and the night has a soundscape of its own. **Milestone 006 (Dangerous Company):** the people speak - Augie, Rourke (glad to see you breathing after all these years) and Eddie greet Danny in their own voices the first time they meet. **The Bird** sings on the Copper Owl's bandstand; walk up to her microphone and the record scratches to a halt and she gives you a piece of her mind. **Lefty Moran**, a North Side gangster, loiters outside Doyle's Garage - leave him be, or shoot at him and he shoots back. People can be wounded and killed now; Danny gets hurt where he is hit (C shows where), and **Doc Pettibone**, the vet on Pruitt Street, patches up gangsters for cash. Danny owns one car; take anyone else's and the beat cop comes after you - if he saw it. M opens the map of the whole ward; the cars can turn round on Marlowe Avenue; their headlights are two beams; and the Morgue stands at the end of Pruitt Street. **Milestone 007 (A Job for Rourke):** Rourke's first job - somebody is squeezing Lindqvist's hardware store without his say-so. Find out who, then settle it your way: talk him round, lean on him, put up your fists (a brass dial over the street: Space in the lit zone, three of five to win) or reach for the gun; then tell Rourke how it went - or not quite. The ward talks, and Rourke finds out what he finds out. **Milestone 008 (Beyond Riverside Ward):** the city goes on. Drive south down Kessler Street into the **Riverfront Yards** - works, warehouses, a coal dock, freight sidings, dim lamps - or west along Marlowe Avenue into **Halloran Square** - flats with warm windows, corner shops, the Bijou picture house, a church and a little square of plane trees - without a stop or a loading screen: each district is built as you come near and let go when you are far away. M opens the **city map**: drag it round with the mouse, zoom with the wheel, R takes you back to yourself. And the police shoot back now: a beat cop who sees you shoot somebody draws his revolver. **Milestone 009 (The City Doesn't Sleep):** Chicago does not wait for Danny. Now and then something happens on its own - two men argue at a kerb, a package changes hands in an alley, the beat cop stops somebody and turns out his pockets - begins, goes on and ends whether you are there or not; walk up and step in, and it ends differently. Not all work comes from Rourke: **Dutch**, outside his night lunch in the Riverfront Yards, has a crate for Novak's Grocery two nights in three, and **Mr. Feeney** of the Bijou in Halloran Square sometimes needs his late show's lost reel found before eleven - your own money, if you take it on in time; leave it and somebody else sees to it. The ward talks, and Rourke hears - nights later, and he only minds his envelopes. **Brennan's coal dump truck** waits on its dock in the yards and **St. Brendan's school bus** at the kerb by the church: slow, heavy, wide in the turn, and somebody else's. The front tyres of the cars now tuck in under their wings. **Milestone 010 (The Darker Side of Chicago):** the city is grittier: litter, coal dust and soot in the streets (thicker in the yards), fences you can see, shadows thrown by the buildings, the cars, the fences and the people, and weather - rain on wet streets with the lamps and headlights shining in it, and fog lying in the streets between the buildings, never over the rooftops. Left Shift runs; in the small hours Danny's eyes grow heavy and the view sways. The city map names every building (by name, or by a made-up address), more as you zoom in, and the minimap rings an incident you can hear and points to one going on out of range. Halloran Square's middle is now a little park, and any bench takes a seat (E; hold Space to let the night pass, never past a moment that matters). Behind the Copper Owl a door leads to the **back room**: No-Limit Texas Hold'em with three regulars and your own money. **Eddie** lends his **Thompson** for a deposit (Tab switches guns; hold the button for a burst) and sells, when a job calls for it, a **parcel**. **Rourke's job at Rosie's Café** in Halloran Square: Vince Duggan's Packard at the kerb, a parcel under it, a bench to watch from, and a short film in the game itself; then the police car, the fire engine and the ambulance. **Milestone 011 (Power & Vice):** the back room has grown: past the card table, a door leads into the Copper Owl's **gaming room** - dark boards, a long shaded lamp over the **wheel** (single-zero roulette: put your chips down with the mouse, Space spins, Leo the croupier marks the number) and three nickel **slot machines** along the wall, the Owl, the Bell and the Star (Space pulls the lever). Behind the wall the band is muffled; the room has its own murmur of talk, chips and glasses. Down at the yards' doorstep, the **Dock & Freight Workers' hall, Local 9**, keeps its lights burning late; across from Halloran Park stands **City Hall** with the mayor's window still lit, and a few doors along **Mayor Dunleavy's** house behind its railing; the aldermen keep their ward offices. A guide to the district: `docs/RIVERSIDE_WARD.md`.

## Run it

Open the project in Godot 4.7.x and press Play (the run scene is `src/main/main.tscn`), or from a terminal:

```bash
godot --path .
```

A new game opens with Danny's monologue (about 23 seconds, with subtitles) while the camera drifts over the ward; any key (E, Space, Enter, Esc or a click) skips it. Then you are in your flat next door to the Copper Owl on Marlowe Avenue; your sedan is parked at the curb outside. A saved game, and every evening after sleeping, starts straight in the flat.

| On foot | | Driving | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| W A S D | walk | W | accelerate |
| Mouse | aim | S | brake, then reverse |
| Left click | shoot (the first click draws the gun) | A / D | steer |
| E | talk / go to bed / go in or out by a door / sit down and get up / get into the nearest parked car (your own sedan, or somebody else's) | Space | handbrake |
| Left Shift | run (held with W A S D) | | |
| Tab | the other gun (your .45, or Eddie's Thompson while you have it) | | |
| Space (held) | sitting on a bench: let the night pass faster | | |
| 1 – 4 | answer when you talk to someone (or click) | E | get out (the car stops first) |
| E or Esc | walk away from a conversation | | |
| Space | in a fistfight: throw the punch when the needle is in the lit zone | | |
| 1 – 4 | at the card table: fold, check / call, bet / raise, all in (wheel or arrows: how much); Esc gets up | | |
| Space | at the card table: on to the next card player's move, the next card or the result | | |
| Mouse | at the wheel: left click puts a chip on the bet under the pointer (a number, the line between two, a crossing, the edge under a column, the boxes outside), right click takes one back | | |

| Space | at a slot machine: pull the lever (one pull a press); Esc steps away | | |

M opens the map of the city (drag it with the mouse, the wheel zooms, R brings it back to Danny) and C how Danny is (where he is hurt); M, C or Esc closes them, and the night waits while they are open. F1 shows the controls card, Esc pauses (Q quits while paused). On the pause screen the arrow keys set the sound levels (Up / Down choose Master, Music, Engine, Effects, Voice or Ambience, Left / Right make it quieter or louder; kept in `user://settings.cfg`). Arrow keys mirror WASD.

### A night's work

Look for the badges over the doors (a cocktail glass, a barber's pole, an apple, a hammer, and the house-shaped badge of your flat) and on the minimap in the bottom-left corner; places off the map wait at its rim in their direction. At a door, E takes you in (or you just walk in).

1. Out of your door, turn west along Marlowe Avenue (on foot or in the sedan) to **Sal's Barber Shop** (red awning, barber's pole). Sal sells the rye his Cicero man brings him: about $1.50 a bottle, dearer when his cupboard is nearly bare, cheaper when it is full, a dime off once you are a regular; six is all you can carry.
2. Take it to **the Copper Owl** (the canopy next door to your flat) and sell it to **Augie** behind the bar: about $3 a bottle, more when his cellar has run dry, less when it is full, and only as many as his cellar has room for and his till can pay. You can also ask for his "insurance" once a night; lean on him and he pays more, remembers it, and pays less for your rye that night.
3. **Novak's Grocery** on the corner of Kessler Street, across from the beat cop: protection money, once a night, as much as his till allows (asked, he keeps back what tomorrow's truck costs; leaned on, he empties it and runs for the copper once you have gone). When his till runs short the truck brings less, his shelves empty and he has less to give; you can lend him money for the truck, and he pays it back with a quarter on top.
4. **Rourke** at the corner table in the Copper Owl: the protection money is his (the purse in the top-left corner shows how much of your money is Rourke's). Hand it over and he gives you your end; or tell him it was a thin night, make up a gap from your own pocket, add a little extra, keep some back, or ask for time. He expects what he believes the ward can pay, and he only knows what he sees from his table and what reaches him as talk — sometimes nights later. Short him too often and he cuts your end, then stops Augie buying your rye, then sends his boys to collect.
5. **Lindqvist Hardware** (south side of Marlowe, green awning): through the door at the back, **Eddie** sells boxes of .45 rounds while his crate lasts. The pistol only reloads from the rounds you carry.
6. **Trouble.** At the Copper Owl the Bird sings at her microphone (E interrupts her; she lets you know about it). Outside Doyle's Garage on Pruitt Street, Lefty Moran minds his own business unless you shoot at him. If you are hurt, C shows where; **Doc Pettibone** at Pettibone Animal Hospital (Pruitt Street, the paw-print badge) patches you up for a few dollars. Go down and you come to on his table, his fee taken. Shoot one of the ward's own people and they are carried off to the Doc's for the night - and nobody forgets it, least of all Rourke.
7. **Rourke's job.** Rourke has work for you: ask him at his table. Ask Mr. Lindqvist who has been collecting from him, ask Eddie what he knows, and find the man outside Doyle's Garage. Talk, threaten or fight (or shoot); then report back to Rourke. The ward map (M) shows what the job needs next and rings the place it concerns.
8. **Cars.** Your own sedan is parked outside your flat. Any other car is somebody's; taking it is theft, and if the beat cop sees you do it he comes to look.
9. **The city's own troubles (M009).** Now and then two men argue at a kerb, a package changes hands in an alley or the beat cop questions somebody - never right in front of you; you come upon them. Walk up and press E to step in: break up the argument or lean on them; ask to see the package, ask for a cut, or (gun drawn) take it; vouch for the man the copper stopped or square the copper with a dollar. Leave them and they settle it themselves; a shot nearby scatters them. Whoever saw it tells the ward.
10. **Your own work (M009).** In the Riverfront Yards, Dutch stands outside his night lunch on Foundry Street: some nights he has a crate for Novak - bring a car up to the kerb, load it (E at the crate) and park outside Novak's before two to be paid; nobody by half past eleven and his nephew takes it. In Halloran Square, Mr. Feeney waits beside the Bijou's marquee: one night in four his late show's reel is lost behind St. Brendan's - bring it to him before eleven. Brennan's dump truck (on the coal dock) and St. Brendan's school bus (Halloran Avenue, by the church) can be driven like any car, slowly; they are not yours.
11. **The back room (M010, M013).** In the Copper Owl, the door in the west wall (beside the bar) leads to the card room. E at the empty chair: choose the stakes (left / right: nickel and dime, dime and quarter, quarter and half), buy in with your own money and play No-Limit Hold'em against Irv (careful), Red (pushy) and Lucky Pete (loose); Moe takes a chair that comes free. Get up between hands with whatever is in front of you; during a hand you are still in, you are asked first (Enter folds and goes, Esc stays). The game breaks up at four. The game goes one step at a time: each card player's move, each new card on the board and each result stays on show until you press Space. The game saves after every hand.
12. **Eddie's Thompson and the parcel (M010).** Ask Eddie for "anything heavier": he lends his Thompson for a $5 deposit ($4 back when you return it; after three nights he wants it back). It has its own rounds; Tab takes it in hand; hold the button down. When Rourke's café job calls for it, Eddie also sells a parcel ($6).
13. **Rosie's Café (M010).** Ask Rourke if he needs anything else taken care of. Duggan parks his green Packard outside Rosie's Café on Marlowe Avenue, Halloran Square, from ten. With Eddie's parcel, E at the car slips it under; a quarter of an hour later it goes. Watch from the bench in front of Halloran Drugs (sit, hold Space) - not from beside the car. Then tell Rourke: $10, once. If a copper saw you at the car, he knows.
14. **The gaming room (M011, M013).** Through the card room's west door. Leo runs the wheel all night, round after round: E at the rail and put chips of your own money on the layout while the bets are open (the brass ring round the wheel runs down to his call, and waits a moment while you are still putting chips down); or just watch (the regulars' bets are called out as they go down). M015: T sets the betting timer's pace - Quick, Normal or Relaxed (Leo's from his next round; remembered between games) - and the table takes chips of 5¢ to $5, up to $5 on a number, $10 on any other inside bet, $50 on an outside bet and $100 a spin (printed under the chips). The ball lands where the rules say and you are paid once (a number 35 to 1, a split 17, a street 11, a corner 8, a line 5, a dozen or column 2, the rest even money; the zero beats every outside bet). The regulars play beside you with their own colour chips. E at any of the three machines: a stake of one, two or five coins (S), Space pulls; M015: after every pull that pays nothing the HOLD lamps light - hold one or two reels (1-3) and pull: only the others spin, at the same stake; then every hold comes off and the next pull spins all three (never two holds in a row). The paytables were lowered to match (with the best holds the machines still keep 4-6 of every hundred cents). Both close at four.
19. **A living gaming room (M013).** Mrs. Halloway, Big Stan, Bertie Sloane, Dolly Crane and Mick Tierney come in through the evening (few early, a full room at midnight, gone by four), play the wheel and the machines, sip their drinks, talk to each other and take a win or a loss as they would. They step away from a machine you come to; the Bell is always free.
20. **Cars stay where you leave them (M013).** A game saved at the wheel loads with you behind it, where it stood, in any district; a car you leave in the street is there when you come back or load the game. Overnight the cars go back to their owners (your own sedan to your door).
15. **The union hall and City Hall (M011, M012).** The Dock & Freight Workers' hall stands at the corner of Wharf Street and Front Street where the yards begin (its cargo-hook badge over the door): walk in, read the noticeboard, look into the business agent's office. City Hall faces Halloran Park across Pruitt Street: its night door at the top of the steps opens on the marble hall (the night clerk at his grille, the roped stairs to the Mayor), the City Clerk's office and the records room behind. Alderman Doran's ward office (his precinct captain in late) and the Regulars' Club (lounge and billiard room) are round the square, Alderman Quilty's office on Marlowe Avenue in the ward (the alderman at his desk till one). The mayor's own house stays private: ring the bell at its gate and his housekeeper says so.
16. **Behind the doors (M012).** A worn brass plate on the pavement marks every street door you can try - warm where it opens, cold where it is shut, amber at a bell - brighter as you come up to it. A thin brass ring on the ground shows what E would act on (a person, a door, a seat, a car). People you know call out to you when you come near them in their room; press E to answer, or walk on. **H** draws your gun and keeps it out (H again puts it away). **Esc** opens the game menu: Resume, Save, Load the last save, the Notebook (what is on your plate tonight), Controls, Sound, Cheats (none yet), Save and quit. A game saved mid-night goes on where and when it was saved - in the street or in a room, in any district.
17. **Freight Doesn't Walk (M012).** In the Riverfront Yards, North Branch Freight's night dispatcher Leon Sobczak (his office on Front Street) has twelve crates that must make the two o'clock truck, and his own freight shed won't load them. Find out why - Walt Haney in the shed, Dutch at the night lunch, the union hall's noticeboard - and settle it your way: talk Leon round, pay what's owed yourself, lean on Walt or take him on with your fists. Ten dollars from Leon when the truck is loaded; leave it, and he sorts it out himself. Your own work, not Rourke's.
18. **After the last song (M012).** Some night after half past one, if you are in the Copper Owl and know the Bird, her last song ends and she asks you to stay. She remembers what you tell her.
19. The clock is at the top of the screen. From 4 am you are told to get home; at dawn the clock stops and waits. Go home and press E at your bed to sleep through to the next evening: money, bottles and rounds stay with you; while you sleep the shops trade, pay their bills and take their deliveries, and the talk goes round.

Progress is saved in `user://chicago1932_save.json` (on Linux `~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/Chicago, 1932/`) after the opening, after every deal, on sleeping and on quitting; delete the file to start over (with the opening). Saves from before M005 load too: the ward and Rourke start fresh around your money and bottles.

## Sound

Rain and fog stay outside the room you are in (M012); on a wet night the rain comes in downpours with thunder now and then, duller indoors. M013: the rain falls down and slants with the wind (its fall no longer runs up or doubles in a downpour), catches the light of lamps and windows, and stays fixed to the street at any window size. The Copper Owl's singer ("Leave the Lights Low") plays all night on one continuous track: through the club's walls it is quieter and muffled (the bass and the beat carry, the words do not), clearer as you come to the door, full and unfiltered inside, and it fades under the city as you walk or drive away; going in and out never restarts it. Under everything: the street's distant traffic (quieter after midnight), dripping alleys, the river and a factory across it, the club's crowd, now and then a far-off horn, a tug on the river or a lid in an alley, the engines of passing cars; doors, shop bells, tills and bottles when you deal; car doors. Indoors the street comes through dull. In the Copper Owl's back rooms (M011) the band comes through the wall, muffled, under the room's own murmur, chips, cards, glasses and the odd laugh; the wheel and the machines have their own clicks, rattles, clunks, coins and bells (all synthesised, all replaceable). Danny's monologue and the song are the user's recordings; how the song was cleaned (its stray noise at the very end trimmed with FFmpeg) is in `assets/audio/README.md`.

## Replace the art and sound

Every image and sound is a `template_` file in `assets/` (or, for the song and the monologue, the user's recording under its own name) that you can override by dropping your own file into `custom/` (in the project while developing, or next to an exported Windows/Linux game, no rebuild needed; restart the game to see or hear it). Sounds can be OGG, WAV or MP3, e.g. `custom/audio/music/copper_owl_jazz.mp3`. The full list with sizes and override paths: `docs/ASSET_SPECS.md`; the rules: `assets/README.md`. The template art is generated by `tools/template_art/` and the template sounds by `tools/template_audio/` (Python with Pillow and numpy; ffmpeg for the M005 sounds). Car bodies leave out the front tyres: they are a separate image (`cars/wheel`) turned by the steering (drawn larger under the truck and the bus).
