# Manual playtest checklist — Milestone 012: The City Behind Closed Doors

Play `src/main/main.tscn` with keyboard, mouse and headphones, ideally on a real graphics card, over a night or two (one of them wet). Tick each item; note problems in `docs/BACKLOG.md` (not as tasks).

## Rooms in every district
- [ ] Walk west over the border into Halloran Square: no pause, no loading. City Hall's night door at the top of the steps on Pruitt Street opens on the marble hall: columns, flags, portraits, benches, the noticeboard, the clock, the night clerk behind his brass grille; the stairs at the east end roped off (E there: BY APPOINTMENT). Through the door at the end of the counter: the City Clerk's office and the records room.
- [ ] Alderman Doran's office (Marlowe Avenue, across from the park): the waiting room and the alderman's own office; the precinct captain talks; the photographs and the desk can be looked at. The Regulars' Club (further west): lounge and billiard room, the steward, smoke - distinct from the Copper Owl and its back rooms.
- [ ] In the ward, Alderman Quilty's office on Marlowe Avenue: Quilty at his desk until one; after one, an empty office.
- [ ] In the Riverfront Yards, North Branch Freight on Front Street: the dispatch office (Leon, the dispatch board) and, at the far end, the freight shed through its loading door on the yard (Walt by the stove, crates everywhere, the held consignment under a tarpaulin). The depot between them stays shut.
- [ ] The mayor's house: the bell at the gate, the housekeeper, no way in.
- [ ] In every room: the roof opens as you step in and closes behind you; the walls and furniture hold; the camera stays smooth; the room's lamps are lit; leaving by E at the door puts you on the pavement facing out.
- [ ] Save inside a room in Halloran Square or the yards (Esc, Save the game), quit, start again: you are in that room at that minute, and can walk out. Walk far away and come back: the room is as you left it (someone struck down there stays down that night; the shed's corner stays empty once the freight has gone).

## Entrances, the highlight, the holster, the menu
- [ ] Brass plates on the pavement before the doors you can try: warm where they open, cold where shut, amber at the mayor's bell; dim from across the street, brighter close up; none at the Copper Owl's inner doors. They never cover a person or a door, and are not floating icons.
- [ ] The thin ring under what E would act on: a person, a door (a line across it), a seat, a car you can get into; nothing while talking; nothing through a wall.
- [ ] H draws your gun and keeps it out; H puts it away; Tab still switches to the Thompson when you carry it; drawing again brings out the one you chose; rounds unchanged.
- [ ] A click on the last answer of a talk, or on Resume in the menu, never fires the gun.
- [ ] Esc: the leather-and-brass menu with its paper page in a period serif; the world stops underneath (the clock too). Notebook shows what is under way tonight in plain words; Controls lists every key; Sound changes the six levels as before; Cheats is a page of none; Save says where and when; Load goes back to the last save; Save and quit closes the game. Esc goes back, then resumes. At a card table, the wheel or a machine, Esc is still the table's.

## The weather
- [ ] In rain and fog, step into any room (in the ward, in Halloran Square, in the yards): no rain falls inside, no fog drifts across the floor; the street outside the building is still wet and foggy. The rain is heard through the walls, duller.
- [ ] On a wet night the rain comes in downpours now and then (thicker, faster, slanting with the wind) and eases off; the downpour has its own heavier sound.
- [ ] Thunder now and then in a downpour - mostly a far roll over the roofs, sometimes a crack overhead - with the street lit cold for a breath; dull and dim from indoors. Never constant.
- [ ] The frame rate in fog and in rain and fog is acceptable on your machine (note it).

## People
- [ ] Walking into the Copper Owl with business on the table, Rourke calls you over ("Mercer. A minute."); answering, he opens on it and you have his usual choices. Augie calls when his cellar is dry, or warns you off after you leaned on him. Eddie calls about his Thompson when it is overdue.
- [ ] Quilty calls as you come in and steps round his desk; walk off and he goes back to his chair. A shooting in the ward reaches him a night later and he mentions it.
- [ ] Nobody calls while you drive, talk, play cards or the wheel, fight, watch a film, or just after a gunshot; nobody calls twice a night; the same greeting is not repeated night after night; a call can simply be ignored. No quest markers, no arrows.
- [ ] After midnight Leon is not at his desk: he is having coffee at Dutch's counter (and can be talked to there), back at half past.

## Freight Doesn't Walk
- [ ] Leon calls out the first time you come into his office, tells you about the Hartmann's crates and the two o'clock truck, and offers ten dollars; you can take it, refuse it, or walk out.
- [ ] Walt won't say why; Dutch will (over coffee), and so will the union hall's noticeboard; the dispatch board and the crates' tag say whose freight it is. The Notebook keeps up.
- [ ] Each way works and feels different: talk Leon round and tell Walt; pay the six yourself; draw your gun and tell Walt to load; fight him (lose, and you can still pay - you are never stuck); or do nothing and Leon pays it himself (and says so). The shed's corner empties when the freight goes; Leon pays ten dollars once.
- [ ] None of it goes through Rourke; days later he may have heard, and says so - no penalty.

## After the Last Song
- [ ] Some night after half past one, in the Copper Owl, having met the Bird: her song ends for good ("That's the last one, folks"), she steps down to the end of the bar and asks you to stay. Walk away and she lets it be (she may ask another night).
- [ ] Answer her: the black bars, the camera drifting from you to her, the club's lamps going down, the room hushed; a few lines; her question; your choice. The film can be skipped to the question. Afterwards the bars slide away and everything is as it was.
- [ ] It feels intimate and quiet, not a romance and not a confession; her recorded voice is unchanged and nothing new is voiced. She greets you afterwards by what you said. It never happens twice.
