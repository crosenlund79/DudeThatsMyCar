# Manual playtest checklist — Milestone 010: The Darker Side of Chicago

Play `src/main/main.tscn` with keyboard, mouse and headphones, ideally on a real graphics card, over two or three nights. Tick each item; note problems in `docs/BACKLOG.md` (not as tasks).

## The dump truck
- [ ] Brennan's coal truck stands at the kerb on Wharf Street in the Riverfront Yards under a lamp, nose towards the ward: you find it driving down from the ward without looking for it. E gets in; it drives (slowly, wide in the turns).
- [ ] The stake trucks in the freight yard are scenery: E at one says the cab is chained up and points you to Brennan's truck on Wharf Street.

## Grit, fences and shadows
- [ ] Streets have litter - newspapers, paper, bottles, flattened boxes, a loose board, rubble, stains - more in the alleys, most in the yards (coal dust, soot), least in Halloran Square. Never on a doorstep, under a lamp or along a fence; never the same piece twice side by side; it reads as dirt, not clutter, and never hides the road.
- [ ] Nothing new is solid: you walk and drive over all of it.
- [ ] Fences are easy to see: board fences, chain-link and iron railings with their posts, standing up from the ground towards the screen edges, their tops clear near the middle. Their collision matches what you see (you stop at the fence, not before it or through it).
- [ ] Buildings throw long soft shadows one way (away from the moon); cars, fences and people have theirs; under a lamp a shadow fades out. The shadows help you read depth and never hide the street.

## Weather
- [ ] The first night is wet: rain falls on screen (under the HUD), the streets look wetter, the lamps' reflections brighter, headlights glare. You hear it outdoors and dully indoors.
- [ ] Later the fog comes in: it lies in the streets between the buildings, banked thicker at their feet, drifting slowly, lit round the lamps and by your headlights - and the rooftops stand clear above it. Never in a room you are in.
- [ ] Rain and fog together look right; the change from one to the other is slow (no snap).
- [ ] In thick fog you can still find your way (the minimap, lamps, lit doors).
- [ ] Sleep: the next night has its own weather.
- [ ] The frame rate in fog is acceptable on your machine (note it).

## Running and tiredness
- [ ] Left Shift with WASD runs; the legs move faster to match. Letting go walks. Shift does nothing in a car, while talking, at the cards, sitting or in a fight.
- [ ] From about half past three the edges of the view darken, the eyelids droop for a moment now and then, the view sways a little (less in a car). Controls still respond; the HUD stays clear; it stops while you talk, play cards or watch the café's film; sleep puts it right.

## The city map and the minimap
- [ ] Zoomed out: districts and landmarks. Medium: businesses and streets. Close up: every building named or given a plain designation and address. No label on top of another; labels stay inside the map.
- [ ] An argument heard from a street away: a ring spreads once on the minimap where it is. Walk out of range: an arrow on the rim while it goes on, gone when it ends. Nothing for a quiet handoff, nothing from another district.

## Halloran Park and sitting
- [ ] The square is a small park: lawns, flowerbeds, hedges, paths crossing, a railing with gates (you walk in by the paths, not over the railing), lamps, benches, somebody strolling.
- [ ] E at a bench sits Danny down; E stands him up in front of it. Holding Space lets the night pass much faster (watch the clock); letting go, normal. Getting hit stands him up.
- [ ] Waiting for the café's moment on the bench, the clock slows down before it - you never miss it.

## The back room and the card game
- [ ] In the Copper Owl, the door in the west wall beside the bar says "Through to The Copper Owl's Back Room". The room feels different: carpet, low green light over the table, smoke, a sideboard; three men at the table, an empty chair.
- [ ] E at the empty chair: the buy-in ($2 / $5 / $10 as you can afford). The table: names and ways of playing, stacks, the dealer's button, the blinds, your cards face up, theirs face down, the board, the pot (and side pots when someone is all in), whose turn (copper glow), what each did.
- [ ] Your turn: 1 fold, 2 check / call, 3 bet / raise (wheel or arrows change it; it never goes below the minimum), 4 all in. The amounts are right; illegal choices are greyed.
- [ ] The card players take a moment to think; the careful one folds a lot, the pushy one raises and bluffs, the loose one calls too much. A hand you win or lose makes sense at the showdown (the winning hand is named).
- [ ] Busted players leave; Moe takes the free chair (once). At four the game breaks up. Esc gets up (mid-hand you fold); your purse is right to the cent afterwards.
- [ ] Chip and card sounds; the club's music muffled through the wall.
- [ ] Workshop (poker pacing): every card player's move stays on show (his plate lit, "Red Gallagher: Raises to $0.60" at the bottom) with a small "Space  Continue" prompt; nothing moves until Space; one press, one move. Holding Space never skips several. Each street of the board turns over on its own press (after an all-in too), and the result waits for Space before the next deal. Space does nothing on your own turn; 1-4 wait while something is on show. It reads at a comfortable pace and is easy to follow.

## Eddie's Thompson and the parcel
- [ ] Ask Eddie for "anything heavier": he lends the Thompson for $5. Tab takes it in hand (a longer gun in the sprite); Tab again, the .45.
- [ ] Held down, it fires in a burst with its own rounds (20 + 40); the aim climbs, the view shakes a little; a sharp report each round; the muzzle flash; it reloads from its own spares; the copper hears it from further off.
- [ ] Sleep and load a game: still on loan, the rounds as you left them. After three nights Eddie asks for it. Give it back: $4 returned.
- [ ] Once Rourke's café job is on, Eddie sells the parcel ($6); it shows beside your purse with its badge. Nothing about it is shown or explained beyond brown paper and string.

## Rosie's Café and the film
- [ ] Rourke (ask if he needs anything else taken care of) explains the job; "not tonight" leaves it open. Accept: Eddie has the parcel.
- [ ] From ten, Duggan's green Packard stands outside Rosie's Café (Marlowe Avenue, Halloran Square, its sign on the roof). E at it slips the parcel under; a quarter of an hour.
- [ ] Sit on the bench in front of Halloran Drugs and hold Space: the clock races, then slows; the film begins - bars, the captions, the camera to the café, a hush, the flash, the street shaking, the fireball, debris, the café's people coming out, a siren, and back to Danny. It feels like the opening's quality: framing, pace, light, sound. E skips it.
- [ ] Afterwards: the wreck burning, smoke rising, the scorch, glass and debris on the street, the onlookers; nothing gory.
- [ ] Standing right by the car when it goes, Danny is hurt. Planting it in sight of a copper: he comes after you later, and the talk says a copper saw somebody.
- [ ] Rourke pays $10 once. The next night the wreck is still there; after that only the scorch. The job is never offered again.

## The emergency
- [ ] Soon after the blast: a siren, the police car comes along Marlowe Avenue from the ward, then the fire engine (its bell), then the ambulance, lamps winking. They stop in a line at the kerb near the wreck.
- [ ] Their crews get out: two coppers on the pavement keeping people back, three firemen at the wreck (the fire goes out a while later, smoke goes on), two ambulance men by their van. They stay for the night; gone the next.
- [ ] You cannot take any of them; you walk round them.
