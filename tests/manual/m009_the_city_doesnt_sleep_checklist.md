# Manual playtest checklist — Milestone 009: The City Doesn't Sleep

Play `src/main/main.tscn` with keyboard, mouse and headphones, ideally on a real graphics card, over two or three nights. Tick each item; note problems in `docs/BACKLOG.md` (not as tasks).

## The city's own troubles
- [ ] Over a night you come upon a few things happening without you: two men arguing at a kerb, a pair in an alley with a package, the beat cop questioning somebody. Not constantly, not the same thing every few minutes, never starting right in front of you.
- [ ] An argument: they face each other and talk (a line over their heads when you are near enough to hear), it gets heated (a shove now and then), then they part - or come to blows. They walk off and are gone.
- [ ] A handoff: one man waits, the other comes along the alley, a small crate changes hands, they go their ways. Come upon one half way through: the second man is already there (it did not start again because you arrived).
- [ ] A police stop: the district's own beat cop walks over to a man on his own legs, questions him, searches him, then lets him go or walks off with him; afterwards he goes back to his round.
- [ ] Walk away from one and come back later: it has moved on or finished without you. In another district, you only ever find them where you are.
- [ ] Step in (E): breaking up an argument ("Fine. Fine!"), leaning on them (they run), asking to see the package (they break it off), a cut ($2), robbing it with the gun drawn ($6 - and a copper who sees it draws on you), vouching for the stopped man (the copper lets him go and comes over to look at you, no gun), a dollar for the copper. Each reads right and ends it differently.
- [ ] A shot nearby: the arguers and dealers break off and run. Shooting one of them ends it.
- [ ] Next night the local who saw it talks about it ("Word is two fellas came to blows outside Sal's"); what you did yourself comes up when you ask Augie, Sal or Novak for news. Talk about you comes before talk about strangers.
- [ ] The lines over their heads are readable at 1280x720 over a lit facade, and never cover the HUD or prompts.

## Dutch's crate (the Riverfront Yards)
- [ ] Dutch (grey shirt, white apron, fair hair) stands outside Dutch's Night Lunch on Foundry Street, his crate by the door, in plain sight (not under the awning).
- [ ] Talk to him: a crate "off a boxcar" for Novak's, four dollars at Novak's door, before two. You can say no; he does not push.
- [ ] Take it on; at the crate on foot with no car about, E tells you it is too heavy. Bring the sedan (or any car but the squad car) up to the kerb: E at the crate puts it in the boot, and you can see it strapped on behind as you drive.
- [ ] Drive up Kessler Street to Novak's, park out front, go in: "I've got a crate out front for you" pays four dollars (or what the till has). The money is yours: the purse in the corner shows nothing more for Rourke.
- [ ] Another night, ignore Dutch: after half past eleven he says his nephew took it. Take it and do not deliver: at two the crate is gone from the boot ("Two o'clock. Novak's shut") and Dutch is sore the next time - but still offers.
- [ ] Two nights after a delivery Rourke (asked "How's business?") has heard you haul crates for the yards, and lets it pass.

## The Bijou's reel (Halloran Square)
- [ ] On night 1 (and every fourth night) Mr. Feeney (midnight-blue dinner jacket, bow tie, sandy hair) waits beside the Bijou's marquee: the late show's reel is lost behind St. Brendan's. On other nights he just advertises the picture.
- [ ] The film can lies in St. Brendan's churchyard (round, tin, BIJOU - LATE chalked on it) and can be reached on foot; E picks it up.
- [ ] Bring it to him before eleven: three dollars and the show goes on. Do nothing and at eleven it is cancelled anyway; if you had promised, you are told, and he says "Some help you were" when you pass.
- [ ] Rourke's job and the ward's business carry on as before whatever you do here.

## Brennan's dump truck and St. Brendan's school bus
- [ ] The dump truck: a 1930s coal truck with a long bonnet, an upright grille, a heaped coal bed and big rear tyres, on Brennan's dock by the river, nose out. The bus: chrome yellow, black rub rails, window rows, SCHOOL BUS on the roof, at the kerb by the church.
- [ ] Both show their nose and tail at a glance; headlights at the front, red tail lamps behind; the front tyres turn with the wheel; a deep, slow engine sound.
- [ ] "Take the Dump Truck (not yours)": taking either is theft; with the beat cop watching, he comes after you.
- [ ] Driving: they gather speed slowly, top out well below a car, steer heavily (the wheel comes round slowly), turn a much wider circle (turning into a side street takes a wide line; turning round takes a three-point turn), but never feel broken. Reversing works.
- [ ] Into a parked car: it is shoved aside, the truck rolls on. Into a wall: it stops at the wall, never through it; a glancing scrape slides along.
- [ ] Traffic stops short of them rather than driving into them.
- [ ] Drive the bus into the ward and back into Halloran Square (or the truck up Kessler Street): no stop, no doubled vehicle, and leaving it in another district it is there when you come back.

## Front tyres
- [ ] Sedan, roadster and squad car: with the wheels straight the front tyres no longer stick out past the wings (a sliver at most, like the rear ones); steering, you see them turn out from under the wings. Check parked cars at several angles, standing and turning.
