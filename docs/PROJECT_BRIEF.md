# Project Brief — Single design and task handoff record

This file **records** decisions and approvals for *Chicago, 1932*; it never grants them. Only the user can approve scope, in their message in the current conversation. **Undecided = ask, don't choose.** The game was converted from a copy of HyperCars; the HyperCars record is archived in `docs/archive/hypercars/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`.

## Game decisions (user-confirmed only)

- **Game title:** *Chicago, 1932* — working title, placeholder (GDD). Repository `DudeThatsMyCar`, an independent game; the original HyperCars repository is never modified from here (user, 2026-11-17).
- **Godot version:** 4.7.x — developed and tested with 4.7.2-stable, GL Compatibility renderer (carried over from HyperCars).
- **Premise / direction:** a top-down, GTA-like gangster sandbox in Prohibition-era Chicago, around 1932, with an original identity (user, kickoff 2026-11-17; `docs/chicago_1932_game_design_document.md` §2). The user's vision: a freely explorable city (streets, intersections, alleys, traffic, pedestrians); responsive 1930s vehicle handling; gangsters, police, rival gangs, Tommy guns, car chases; bootlegging, robberies, rackets, speakeasies, criminal jobs; walking, getting in and out of vehicles, basic interactions; police attention, money, reputation, consequences; a living city with dark humour and period atmosphere; fun even when just driving around. *This is direction, not approval: every feature needs its own approved objective.* The GDD's other sections are proposals.
- **Perspective / art direction:** true top-down gameplay camera with enough depth to show building height and facades, never an extreme perspective that hides the roads (user, M001). Hand-painted, slightly whimsical HyperCars visual identity with a convincing 1930s urban atmosphere (user, M001; HyperCars approval 2026-11-14). Style reference: the user's drawings in `docs/art_refrence/` — study their characteristics only (soft brushwork, organic outlines, imperfect shapes, soft shading, colour, proportions); never recreate the depicted objects. `template_` graphics must be proper, coherent art, not debug shapes, consistent so the user's art can replace them one at a time through the `custom/` override contract, including next to an exported Windows/Linux game without rebuilding.
- **The nighttime henchman (user, M002, approved direction):** the protagonist is a henchman who works through the night and sleeps by day. The whole playable game takes place at night for now — no daytime rendering, no day / night cycle (M003 added a night clock and sleeping through the day, still night only). The long-term atmosphere is "dark and darker": lively lit nightlife, quiet midnight streets, threatening late-night alleys. Riverside Ward's night look is the visual foundation; do not redesign the neighbourhood or regenerate its artwork unnecessarily.
- **Controls (user, M001, M002; M003, M004 additions):** on foot — WASD move, mouse aims independently, left click shoots, E interact (talk, go to bed, go in or out by a door) / get into a car; while talking, 1–4 (or a click) answer, E or Esc walk away. Driving — W accelerate, S brake / reverse, A / D steer, Space handbrake, E get out. M006: M opens and closes the ward map (user; Esc also closes it); C the status screen (key chosen by Claude, "a suitable key").
- **Driving (user, M001, M002):** satisfying, accessible city driving, not a simulator: the HyperCars core kept, with weight (gradual acceleration), steering that builds rather than snaps, low-speed precision for streets, alleys and parking, glancing wall contact that slides with appropriate momentum (never through solid objects), intuitive reverse / braking / handbrake, meaningful Sedan / Roadster differences; targeted, configurable changes rather than a new model. Exits never into walls or inaccessible places. **M003 correction (user): "This is NOT a racing game"** — cars must look and behave like normal road vehicles: natural, progressive turning with a believable turning radius, smooth acceleration and deceleration for heavy 1930s cars, sensible low-speed manoeuvring and reversing, visible wheel steering and convincing rotation through turns, forgiving glancing collisions with buildings, no instantaneous direction changes or racing-style handling; responsiveness preserved, believable urban driving first.
- **Handgun (user, M002):** one simple period handgun: visible gun and muzzle flash, bullets with sensible speed, range and collision, a period gunshot sound, simple ammunition without an inventory, shots blocked by buildings and solid obstacles, basic impact feedback. No other weapons, shops or upgrades yet. M003: basic ammunition purchase from a back-room dealer (no weapon catalogue).
- **Police (user, M002, experimental):** one visible officer who idles or patrols near a street corner, reacts visibly to nearby gunfire and pursues, navigates round buildings, can be escaped on foot or by car, does not know where the player is forever, gives up after the player stays undetected a while and returns to his patrol. Not yet: wanted levels, reinforcements, police cars, arrests, police gunfights, citywide searches. M003 (user): a recognisable 1930s police car parked in an alley; the officer's gunfire reaction and pursuit preserved; no police vehicle AI, no full wanted system.
- **Atmosphere (user, M001):** start at night; dense brick buildings with flat roofs and recognisable rooftop details, fire escapes, narrow spaces between buildings, lit windows, intersections, sidewalks, curbs, back alleys, period street lamps on wet reflective streets, subtle smoke / steam / shadows; a speakeasy / jazz club, a garage and a warehouse; a few parked 1930s cars; handcrafted, intentionally designed, part of a larger neighbourhood rather than an enclosed course.
- **A night's work (user, M003):** the Riverside Ward becomes a small functioning criminal neighbourhood, "a small, charming, playable experience — not a giant simulation". Enterable businesses whose roof becomes transparent while you are inside, with wall / door / furniture collisions, E prompts and a reusable, simple, modular interior approach (first the Copper Owl: bar, tables, lighting, bartender). A small criminal economy: money, small item quantities, simple dialogue with 1932 Chicago personality (no generic debug menus, no elaborate dialogue engine): buy bootleg liquor from a barber a short way down the street and sell it to the Copper Owl's bartender for more; protection money from the bartender and a grocer (believable reactions and consequences); a hardware store with a back-room gun dealer selling basic ammunition; prices, inventory and money persistent where practical; no unlimited money from repeating an interaction. An inhabited ward: a handful of pedestrians on the sidewalks, sparse civilian cars on believable lanes with simple intersection behaviour and basic collision avoidance, pedestrians reacting to approaching cars, activity varying modestly by hour (no crowds or heavy traffic late at night, behaviour over quantity, no citywide simulation). Time and sleeping: a visible clock near the top of the screen, every game begins in the evening, time advances, a configurable late hour tells the player to go home, a small modest apartment with a bed, sleeping advances to the following evening restoring the night's starting state while keeping money and items; no daytime graphics or gameplay, never forced to sleep abruptly.
- **Street smarts (user, M004):** a focused usability and visual-polish milestone. Cars must show at once which way they face, even with the headlights off (clear front and rear, believable 1930s silhouettes, no misleading front-end width; visible steering and the M003 handling kept; a visual correction, not a physics redesign). The grocery entrance must be verified and made discoverable. Clear, tasteful markers over the entrances of the accessible places (the Copper Owl, the barbershop, the grocery, the hardware store and the apartment), more noticeable as the player approaches on foot or in a car, the apartment distinct from the businesses; markers identify places, they do not imply quests. A small, readable minimap in a screen corner: nearby streets, blocks and geography, the player's position and facing, icons for the accessible places (the same meanings as the world markers), police presence only where it exists (no invented locations), correct while walking and driving, readable at 1280×720, in the hand-painted 1930s style, never covering the clock, HUD or prompts. Not in M004: new missions or activities, new buildings, weapons or police systems, major traffic or pedestrian AI changes, new driving physics, a navigation / GPS system, daytime.
- **The city has ears (user, M005):** a living gangster neighbourhood, not scripted missions. The protagonist is Danny "Dimes" Mercer, who works for the boss **Rourke**. The businesses have money, stock, demand, running costs and relationships and evolve on their own, overnight too; shortages change what they do and what they can pay; trading restores them; modest, deterministic and testable, with no unlimited-money exploits and no arbitrary random punishments. Rourke is a persistent person at an existing location, distinct from Eddie, whose expectations follow the local economy, earlier earnings, Danny's reliability and only what he has actually learnt (he does not know every transaction); Danny can pay, negotiate, explain shortages, hold back or add extra; pressure and lasting consequences, never a game over. Danny's own profits (the rye trade, modest arrangements) are distinct from the protection owed to Rourke and from deliberate skimming; no mission boards, quest markers or morality meters. People remember threats, favours, payment disputes, good arrangements, dishonesty and broken promises; news spreads through believable local talk; at least one emergent incident from business circumstances that Danny can ignore, exploit or resolve; local and lightweight, no citywide faction simulation. **Audio is a major priority:** extend the existing resolver and `custom/audio/` overrides (OGG / WAV / MP3, working beside an exported Windows/Linux game without rebuilding, graceful fallback, `custom/` never overwritten); the user's two recordings in `assets/audio/_inbox/` — *Danny Dimes Mercer - Opening Monologue* as the opening narration of a new game over a lightweight cinematic view of the living ward (night ambience underneath, other audio ducked, the voice preserved exactly, its real length, optional verified subtitles, skippable, a smooth hand-off, never on loading a save or after sleeping, a missing file never blocks), and *Copper Owl Bird - Leave the lights low* as in-world music at the Copper Owl (full, warm and unfiltered inside; outside low-passed and quieter from inside the building, clearer on approach, no abrupt changes, no restart or doubling on entering or leaving, fading under the city when driving away, looping sensibly, replaceable e.g. as `custom/audio/music/copper_owl_jazz.mp3`) — with the original master never artificially altered except the requested cleaning: its unwanted noise in the last two seconds trimmed (FFmpeg, documented parameters), the `_inbox` original untouched; the old HyperCars audio in `_inbox` untouched and unused. A living night soundscape (streets, back alleys, the river, the Copper Owl's room, business and vehicle sounds) varying with place, proximity, inside / outside and the hour, with modest replaceable `template_` sounds; volume controls for Master, Music, Engine, Effects, Voice and a new Ambience, persisted. Preserve the gameplay, graphics, driving, night-only setting, interiors and controls; no new districts, elaborate weapons, large-scale combat or daytime.
- **Dangerous company (user, M006):** make the Riverside Ward more dangerous, personal and alive while preserving the night atmosphere, driving, economy, Rourke, memory and gossip, music and the narrated opening. The user's six character recordings in `assets/audio/_inbox/` (the Bird interrupted on stage; first-meeting greetings of Rourke — who recognises Mercer back after a long absence — the Bird, Augie and Eddie; Eddie's line on first showing his wares) integrated through the override system, originals untouched, never invented replacements: greetings on first meeting only (remembered across saves), Eddie's sales line on first access to his merchandise, no overlapping voices, music and ambience ducked under speech, missing recordings harmless. **The Bird**, a recognisable female performer on the Copper Owl's stage who performs to the song; E interrupts her (a brief record scratch, the song stops, her complaint), a simple conversation, her first-meeting greeting as a separate event never over the complaint, then her performance resumes; no relationship or romance system. Walking animation matched to walking speed (animation timing, not movement speed). **One more gangster**, distinctive, neutral, met in normal exploration, hostile only if attacked, visibly hit, can be injured and killed; no gang or faction system. **Human injury and mortality**: a modest reusable damage model; NPCs can be wounded and killed, the dead stop moving and interacting, injury and death look different, no excessive gore, no repeated rewards or talk from the dead; consequences for essential named characters only by a deliberate, documented rule (no silent respawn, no destroyed economy). **Vehicle ownership**: Mercer owns one car; taking another is theft; police investigate only a theft an officer could witness; one incident per stolen car; shooting behaviour preserved; no registration or insurance system. **A larger map on M** (whole ward, same geography and icons, readable, M / Esc close, no shooting or movement meanwhile; no GPS or quest tracking). **U-turns** possible on the street in some suitable places without arcade handling or undoing M003. **A character status screen** (foundation only: a silhouette by region — head, torso, arms, legs — showing where he is hurt; no inventory, armour, equipment or complex medicine). **The local veterinarian** who patches up gangsters for payment (healing shown on the status screen; no treatment animations or minigame). **The Morgue** as an atmospheric landmark only (no gameplay). **Seamless district loading: investigation only.** **Headlights**: two distinct beams that widen with distance, plausible and efficient. New art as replaceable `template_` assets; no unrelated artwork regenerated.
- **Not yet (user, M002, M003):** pedestrian crowds, heavy traffic, police vehicle AI, a full wanted system, more weapons, full combat AI, criminal factions, missions, reputation, complex inventories, giant NPC systems, expanded districts, daytime, daily schedules, citywide simulation.
