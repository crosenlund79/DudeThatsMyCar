## Streets and places

- **Marlowe Avenue** (east–west, wide, cobbled streetcar tracks down the middle) — the main street: shops with awnings, the **Copper Owl** supper club (dark green tile, copper trim, neon owl on the roof, a lit canopy over the sidewalk) and, across the street, the six-storey **Hotel Marlowe**. You start at the streetcar stop between them.
- **Calloway Street** and **Pruitt Street** (east–west), **Dorsey**, **Kessler** and **Wharf Street** (north–south): flats (red brick and greystone), shops, a few warehouses.
- **Alleys** run east–west through the middle of the central blocks, brick-paved, lined with fire escapes, back doors, ash cans; behind the Copper Owl, a stack of crates and barrels from tonight's delivery.
- **Gangways**: narrow passages between houses (about 26 px). A person fits; a car does not.
- **The beat cop** walks a short round on the north side of Marlowe Avenue by the Kessler Street corner, between the newsstand and his police call box (M002). Shots within earshot bring him running; get out of his sight and he searches, gives up and goes back to his round.
- **Doyle's Garage** on Pruitt Street, with its forecourt, a gas pump and two parked cars.
- **Lakeshore Cold Storage**, a four-storey warehouse on the river at Wharf Street, with a loading apron along the quay (crates, barrels, a parked sedan).
- **The river** along the east side; the **Marlowe Avenue Bridge** is raised for the night (both bascule leaves up), behind gates with red lanterns. Lamps and lit windows on the far bank.

## Rooms you can walk into (Milestone 003)

Doors with warm light on the pavement can be walked through (or press E at the door; E again by the door inside takes you out). Since Milestone 004 a painted badge floats over the main door of each place — a cocktail glass for the Copper Owl, a barber's pole for Sal's, an apple for Novak's, a hammer for Lindqvist's, and a teal house-shaped badge for your flat — faint from a block away, clearer as you come near, pulsing at the door; the same badges are on the minimap. Inside, the building's roof and walls fade away and the room shows from above, lit by its own lamps; it closes up again as you leave. E talks to whoever is there; answer with 1–4 (or a click), E or Esc walks away.

- **Your Flat** — one ground-floor room in the Marlowe Flats, next door to the Copper Owl: iron bed, rag rug, radio, wardrobe, kitchen table, stove. Every night begins here at 7:30 pm; E at the bed sleeps through to the next evening. Your sedan is parked at the curb outside.
- **The Copper Owl** — front door under the canopy, back door to the alley where the bootleg delivery stands. The bar along the back wall with **Augie** behind it, stools, tables with guests, a baby grand on the bandstand, and **Rourke** at the corner table (M005). Augie buys bootleg rye (as many bottles as his cellar has room for and his till can pay) and can be asked — or leaned on — for protection money once a night.
- **Sal's Barber Shop** — a block west on the north side of Marlowe Avenue (red awning, barber's pole, painted roof sign). Two chairs before the mirror, a bench, and the cupboard where **Sal** keeps the rye he sells you (what his supplier brought, up to ten bottles).
- **Novak's Grocery** — on the corner of Kessler Street, south side of Marlowe, across from the beat cop: shelves of tins, produce crates, the counter and till. **Mr. Novak** pays protection once a night; lean on him and he runs out for the copper once you have gone.
- **Lindqvist Hardware** — south side of Marlowe, west of Kessler (green awning). **Mr. Lindqvist** at the front counter; through the inner door, **Eddie**'s back room with a card table and a crate of pistols. Eddie sells boxes of .45 rounds, nothing more.

## Beyond the ward (Milestone 008)

The ward is no longer alone: two neighbouring districts lie beside it in the same world, and you drive or walk into them without a stop.

- **The Riverfront Yards** (south, `src/city/riverfront_yards.gd`): Kessler and Wharf Street run on south past Pruitt Street (their barricades are gone; Dorsey Street is still closed). Front Street and Foundry Street run east-west to the river, which carries on south with its far bank out of reach. The Great Lakes Tanning Co. (a factory with tall steel windows) and its smoking stack in a fenced yard; North Branch Freight's depot and its loading yard with parked trucks; Brennan Coal & Ice on its dock by the river; a cooperage, a boiler house and Dutch's Night Lunch, lit for the shift, along Foundry Street; behind a board fence with a gate on Wharf Street, the railroad's sidings with boxcars; the embankment closes the south. Fewer, dimmer lamps; a couple of workmen and one car about; a beat cop on Front Street by the freight depot; two parked cars that belong to somebody.
- **Halloran Square** (west, `src/city/halloran_square.gd`): Marlowe Avenue (with the streetcar line) and Pruitt Street run on west past Dorsey Street (Calloway Street is still barricaded). Halloran Avenue, Garrity and Ashby Street; dense flats of buff common brick and greystone with many warm windows and washing on the roofs; corner shops with awnings - Kowalski Bakery and Halloran Drugs; the Bijou picture house on Marlowe Avenue under its own lit canopy; St. Brendan's church and tower on the corner; the square: gravel walks under plane trees, two benches, its own lamps. Six people and two cars about, a beat cop by the square, three parked cars. Barricades close its west and south ends, the embankment its north.
- **Seamless.** A neighbouring district is built only as you come near its border (about 1300 px), a few milliseconds a frame, and is there complete before you reach it; until it is, the way in is closed. Only two districts are kept at a time - the ward and the one you are in or heading for; one you leave far behind is let go (its cars - including any you left there - are put away exactly where they were, and come back when you return). The HUD's top-left corner names the district you are in. The beat cops keep to their own district: chased across a border, a copper watches you from the edge and then gives up as usual.
- **The police shoot back.** Gunfire a beat cop only hears gets the old chase. But if he sees you shoot somebody, or you shoot at him, he draws his .38 revolver ("The copper draws his revolver!", and the pursuit line turns to THE COPPER IS SHOOTING AT YOU): he keeps a gunfight's distance and fires every two seconds or so after a moment to aim, only when he has a clear shot - never through a building, never into a car or a person in the way (he steps aside instead). His rounds wound you where they land; a round through your car's door now and then. Lose him as before; when you go down, he stands down. Passers-by never carry guns; Lefty still only shoots back when provoked.
- **The city map** (M): every district in its place with dashed borders and its name, the streets, the landmarks, the places you can walk into, your car, every beat cop and you. Drag with the mouse to look round, the wheel zooms between street level and the whole city, R (or the button in the key) takes you back to yourself. You can look at a district without going there. The small map in the corner runs on across the borders.

## The city doesn't sleep (Milestone 009)

- **The city's own troubles** (`src/incidents/`). A few times a night, somewhere in the three districts, something happens without Danny: an **argument** between two men at a kerb (talking, getting heated - a shove now and then - and parting, or coming to blows), a **handoff** in a quiet alley (one man waits, the other comes along, a package changes hands, they go) or a **police stop** (the district's own beat cop walks to a man, questions him, turns out his pockets, then lets him go or takes him in). Each begins at its time, goes through its phases on the night clock and ends - whether or not anyone is watching; in a district that is not built it is only a few numbers. It never begins right where Danny is (it waits a little, or happens elsewhere), never two at once on the same stretch of pavement, at most two at a time, and the same night always has the same plan (a reloaded night does not repeat what is over). The places: the ward's kerbs outside the Copper Owl, Novak's and Sal's, the alleys behind the Copper Owl and the Hotel Marlowe, the beat cop's corner; the yards' depot gate, Dutch's, the works lane, the coal dock and Front Street; the Bijou, Kowalski's, the alley off the square, St. Brendan's churchyard and the square.
- **Stepping in.** Walk up to them and press E ("Step in", "Walk over", "Have a word with the officer"): break up the argument (they part, muttering) or lean on them (they run); ask to see the package (they break it off), ask for a cut (two dollars) or - gun in hand - take it (six dollars, and a copper who sees it draws on you); vouch for the man the copper stopped (he lets him go and gives Danny a hard look) or square the copper with a dollar. A shot nearby scatters an argument or a handoff; a man shot ends it. The local who saw it - Augie, Novak, Sal, Dutch or Mr. Feeney - tells the ward, and remembers Danny's part; Rourke hears only what reaches Augie, nights later, and remarks on it ("Your own time's your own business, Dimes - so long as it don't bring coppers to my door").
- **Dutch's crate** (`src/opportunities/`). Two nights in three, Dutch - grey shirt, white apron - stands outside his night lunch on Foundry Street with a crate "off a boxcar" for Novak's Grocery, beside his door. Take it on, bring a car up to the kerb and press E at the crate: it goes into the boot (you can see it strapped on behind). Park outside Novak's and Novak pays four dollars from his till (what the till has), and has some tins on his shelves for it. Nobody has taken it by half past eleven: Dutch sends it with his nephew. Taken and not delivered by two: the nephew fetches it out of your boot, and Dutch remembers (he will still offer you the next one, warily).
- **The Bijou's reel.** One night in four the film exchange's boy loses the late show's reel. Mr. Feeney, the Bijou's manager - midnight-blue dinner jacket, bow tie - waits beside the marquee and cannot leave his lobby: the can lies in St. Brendan's churchyard. Bring it to him before eleven and the show goes on (three dollars); otherwise it is cancelled at eleven all the same, and the square talks about it. Either way the next night is a new one; neither touches Rourke's job.
- **Danny's own money.** What Dutch's crate, the Bijou and the city's handoffs bring in is Danny's: nothing is set aside for Rourke and Rourke expects no more for it. Rourke does not know what Danny earns on his own unless the talk carries it to him, and he does not hold it against him.
- **Brennan's dump truck and St. Brendan's school bus.** The yards' coal truck stands on its dock, nose out towards Front Street; the parish school bus at the kerb on Halloran Avenue beside the church, facing south. Both are somebody else's (a theft like any car, and a copper who sees it notices), and both drive like what they are: slow to gather speed and slower at the top (about 34 and 38 mph), heavy steering that comes round slowly, a turning circle half as wide again as a car's (turn into a side street wide; turning round in a street takes a three-point turn), a long box of a body, and the weight to shove a parked car out of the way. You can drive them into the ward and back; they are never doubled.

## The darker side of Chicago (Milestone 010)

- **Grit.** Litter lies where people drop it - newspapers, paper, bottles, flattened boxes, a loose board, brick rubble, worn stains, and in the yards coal dust and soot - thickest in the Riverfront Yards and thinnest in Halloran Square; never on a doorstep, a fence line or under a lamp, never twice the same, and never in the way (it is painted on the ground; nothing new is solid). Fences stand up from the ground with posts you can see: board fences, chain-link and iron railings. Every building throws a long shadow away from the moon; cars, fences and people have theirs; under a lamp a shadow fades into the light.
- **Weather.** Four states: clear, rain, fog, rain and fog. Rain falls on screen, the streets shine wetter and the lamps' reflections brighten, headlights glare, and the rain is heard (dully indoors). Fog lies in the streets between the buildings - drifting, banked thicker at the buildings' feet, lit by the lamps and headlights - and never over the rooftops or in a room. The weather changes slowly through the night; each night has its own (the first is wet; the same night always has the same).
- **Running and tiredness.** Left Shift runs (on foot). From half past three Danny tires: the edges of the view darken, his eyelids droop for a moment now and then, the view drifts a little (less when driving) - never over the HUD, never while talking, at the cards or in a film; sleep puts it right.
- **The city map's names.** Zoomed out: districts and landmarks. Closer: the businesses and the streets. Close up: every building - its own name, or what it is and a made-up address ("Flats, 2214 Marlowe Ave") - none written over another. **Incident pings:** an argument loud enough to hear rings once on the minimap where it is; one going on out of range in this district gets an arrow on the rim until it ends. A quiet handoff is not given away, nor anything in another district.
- **Halloran Park.** The square's middle is a little park: four lawns, flowerbeds, hedges, paths crossing in the middle, an iron railing with gates where the paths come in, lamps and benches, a stroller. **Any bench takes a seat**: E sits down (and stands up in front of it), and holding Space lets the night pass twenty times faster - slowing to normal before anything that must be seen (the café's moment); being hit stands him up.
- **The back room** (`src/poker/`). Through the door in the Copper Owl's west wall (it is the back of the building next door): worn carpet, a green-shaded lamp low over the card table, a sideboard of bottles, cigar smoke. Irv Feldman (careful), Red Gallagher (pushy) and Lucky Pete (loose) play No-Limit Texas Hold'em every night with their own money; Moe Brandt takes a chair that comes free, once a night. E at the empty chair: buy in for $2, $5 or $10; blinds a nickel and a dime; no house cut. The full rules - blinds and the button (heads-up too), four betting rounds, minimum bets and raises, short all-ins that do not reopen the betting, side pots, split pots and the odd chip, busting. Get up between hands with what is in front of you (mid-hand you fold); the game breaks up at four; a hand left unfinished by quitting the game counts for nothing. One thing at a time (workshop, poker pacing): each card player's move (his plate lit, the line at the bottom naming him), each street of the board (the flop, the turn, the river - one at a time, even when an all-in deals them all at once) and each result stays on show with a small "Space  Continue" prompt until Space is pressed; one press, one step, and holding Space never skips more; the betting keys wait while something is on show.
- **Eddie's Thompson and the parcel.** Eddie lends his Thompson for a $5 deposit and keeps a dollar of it when it comes back; after three nights he asks for it. It has its own stick of twenty and forty spare rounds; Tab switches between it and the .45; held down it fires about ten rounds a second and climbs; it is heard further off than a pistol. The parcel - a shoebox in brown paper and string, nothing more is shown - Eddie sells for $6 only once Rourke's café job is on.
- **Rosie's Café.** Rourke's second job: Vince Duggan, who sells beer off Rourke's trucks in Halloran Square, takes his coffee at Rosie's Café on Marlowe Avenue (the far west end, by the barricade) every night from ten, his green Packard at the kerb. Rourke wants the car gone and nobody hurt. With the parcel, E at the Packard; a quarter of an hour later it goes, once. From the bench in front of Halloran Drugs (or anywhere within about 700 px) a short film plays in the game: the bars, Duggan at his coffee, a hush, the flash, every window on the block, the café's people coming out, a siren; E skips it (nothing is skipped in the world). The wreck burns at the kerb with a column of smoke; the police car, the fire engine and the ambulance drive in along Marlowe Avenue from the ward, stop in a line and stay the night, their crews round the wreck; the firemen put the fire out. The wreck stays two nights, the scorch for good. Nobody knows it was Danny unless a copper saw him at the car; standing too close, he is hurt. Rourke pays $10 once.

## Power & Vice (Milestone 011)

- **The gaming room** (`src/gaming/`). Past the card table, a door in the card room's west wall leads into the ground floor of the house next door (flats above; from the street it still looks like a shuttered shop). Dark stained boards, a long shaded lamp low over the wheel, three nickel machines along the back wall, two small marble-topped tables with drinks and ashtrays, old racing prints and a tall clock, a sideboard of bottles, smoke under the ceiling; Leo Varga the croupier at the wheel (green eyeshade), a woman at the rail, a man across the table, a clerk with his drink. The card room and its game are as they were.
- **The wheel.** Single-zero European roulette, the pockets in the real order. E at the rail (Leo must be there; the wheel closes at four). Chips of 5¢, 10¢, 25¢ and $1 from your own money (1-4 or the mouse wheel); a left click puts the chip on the bet under the pointer - a number, the line between two (split), a crossing of four (corner), the outer edge under a column of three (street) or between two columns (line of six), the zero's edge (0-1-2, 0-2-3, the first four), or the boxes outside (dozens, columns, 1-18, even, red, black, odd, 19-36) - and the numbers it covers light up with what it pays; a right click takes a chip back, Backspace all of them, R puts the last spin's bets down again. Space: "no more bets" - the number is decided then; the head turns, the ball runs round the track, drops, rattles over the frets and comes to rest in that number's pocket; Leo rings his bell and marks it with the brass dolly; winners are paid (35, 17, 11, 8, 5, 2 or 1 to 1, stake back), the rest raked. Limits: 5¢ a bet at least; 50¢ on a number, $1 on another inside bet, $5 outside, $10 a spin. The last twelve numbers are kept. Walking away mid-spin settles it at once. Now and then Leo spins for the others (the head turns, you hear the ball).
- **The machines.** The Owl (copper), the Bell (red) and the Star (green): three reels of cherries, lemons, oranges, plums, bells, bars and sevens. E at one, Space pulls the lever (5¢; the Star 10¢): the reels run and stop left to right with a clunk each; what shows on the line is paid by the paytable printed on the front (a cherry on the left reel up to bars or sevens), the coins dropping into the cup and the bell for a good win. Nothing is decided by your money or your luck so far: every stop comes up alike.
- **Sound.** In the back rooms the Copper Owl's band comes through the wall, low and muffled, under the room's own murmur; now and then a laugh, chips being stacked, a shuffle, glasses, a chair. Step back into the club and the band is full again.
- **The union hall.** Dock & Freight Workers, Local 9: a two-storey brick hall at the corner of Wharf Street and Front Street, where the Riverfront Yards begin (its cargo-hook badge over the door on Wharf Street, its name on the roof and on the city map, a lamp by the door). Inside: benches where the men wait, the noticeboard (E reads it: shift lists, a meeting notice, dues, a clipping), a pot-bellied stove; through a doorway the business agent's office - his desk under a green lamp, filing cabinets, the safe, a map of the docks, the local's charter. Nothing to join, nothing to do: a place to look round.
- **City Hall and the aldermen.** City Hall faces Halloran Park across Pruitt Street: limestone, round-headed windows, a copper cupola on the roof, granite steps between two globe lamps. Mayor Dunleavy's house stands a few doors along, set back behind a little garden and an iron railing, its curtains lit (on the map with its address). Alderman Doran's ward office is across Marlowe Avenue from the park, the Regulars' Club (the precinct men) further west on the avenue; in the ward, Alderman Quilty's office on Marlowe Avenue. All shut at night: E at a door tries it and says why (City Hall: the mayor's window still lit). No elections, no favours, no politics to play - yet.

## The city behind closed doors (Milestone 012)

- **Rooms in every district.** The rooms of Halloran Square and the Riverfront Yards are built with their district (`src/city/halloran_interiors.gd`, `src/city/yards_interiors.gd`, written in the district's own coordinates) and let go with it; the district Danny stands in is never let go, so a room never disappears under him. A game saved mid-night goes on where and when it was saved, in the street or in any room.
- **City Hall** (Halloran Square, Pruitt Street): the night door at the top of the steps opens on the marble hall - four columns, two flags, mayors past in oils, benches, the noticeboard, a tall clock and, across the back, the night clerk behind his counter's brass grille. The broad stair at the east end goes up to the Mayor's office: a velvet rope, BY APPOINTMENT. Through the door at the end of the counter, the City Clerk's office (a desk, filing cabinets, the vault's iron door) and the records room (bound ledgers: births, deaths, permits, the ward rolls).
- **Alderman Doran's ward office** (Marlowe Avenue, across from the park): the waiting room - chairs along the wall, a horsehair couch under the photographs, the precinct captain's desk - and through a doorway the alderman's own office: his desk, the flag, his portrait, the cabinets and the safe. Doran is out; his precinct captain keeps the lamp lit.
- **The Regulars' Club** (Marlowe Avenue, further west): the lounge - cracked leather armchairs round small tables, a couch, the steward's sideboard, old Mike Halloran in oils - and the billiard room under its green lamp. Members and their guests; nobody gambles and nobody joins tonight.
- **Alderman Quilty's office** (the ward, Marlowe Avenue): one modest room - his desk under a green lamp, the visitors' chairs, the ward map in red pencil, the photographs, a stove, last campaign's handbills. Quilty is in until one, then goes home.
- **North Branch Freight** (the yards, Front Street): the night **dispatch office** at the west end of the depot (the dispatch board, Leon Sobczak's desk and telephone, a bench for the drivers, a stove) and the **freight shed** at the east end, its wide door on the loading yard (stacked crates, a hand truck, the shed gang's stove, Walt Haney - and, while Leon's trouble lasts, twelve crates under a tarpaulin). The rest of the depot stays shut between them.
- **The mayor's house** stays private: the bell at its gate brings the housekeeper down the path to say so.
- **Entrances and the highlight.** A brass plate on the pavement before each street door Danny can try (warm: opens; cold: shut for the night; amber: a bell); a thin ring on the ground under what E would act on.
- **People who speak first.** Rourke, Augie, Eddie, the Bird, Quilty and Leon call out when Danny comes near in their room, from what they remember and what the talk has told them; E answers, walking on lets it be.
- **Freight Doesn't Walk** and **After the Last Song**: see the README and `docs/M012_PERFORMANCE_SCRIPT.md`.
- **Weather.** Rain and fog stay out of the room Danny is in (its building), in every district; wet nights bring downpours and, now and then, thunder.

## A job for Rourke (Milestone 007)

- **The job.** At his corner table Rourke has work for Danny ("You said you had work, Mr. Rourke?" takes the place of "How's business?"): somebody has been collecting at Lindqvist's hardware store without his say-so. Danny can take it, put it off (a night at a time; the third time Rourke gives it to somebody who shows up) or turn it down (Rourke is disappointed, not an enemy). If Lefty Moran is already dead when Rourke would bring it up, there is no job: somebody saved him the trouble.
- **Finding out.** Mr. Lindqvist, asked, names the man: Lefty Moran, eight dollars every Friday for a month, outside Doyle's Garage. Eddie in the back room knows how Lefty fights (it helps with the dial and with a threat). The ward map (M) shows the job in a few words under the key and a thin copper ring round the place it concerns now - the Copper Owl, the hardware store or Lefty's corner - and Lefty's corner once Danny knows his name. Each step also shows as a notice.
- **Settling it.** Lefty does not start anything. Danny can **talk** ("Keep what you took. Just stop." - a deal; asking for Rourke's money back gets laughed off), **threaten** him (it works with the gun out, or knowing how he fights, or with Lefty already hurt; badly hurt himself, or after losing to him, Danny's word carries little; a called bluff cannot be tried again that night) or **fight**: "Put 'em up, Lefty." Shooting him is always possible, under the M006 rules (he shoots back; killed, he stays dead, and that settles it too); wounded, he will not talk any more. Leaned on or beaten, Lefty hands over twenty dollars.
- **The fistfight.** A brass dial comes up over the street above the two men (no zoom, no other screen): a needle sweeps from the top, a cream zone is lit somewhere round the dial; press **Space** while the needle is in the zone. Five checks, three to win; the zone moves and the needle's pace varies a little each time; a gold flash and a thud when a punch lands, a red flash when Danny takes one. While it lasts Danny cannot walk, shoot or open the maps, and nobody else can start a fight. Nobody dies in a fistfight: the loser takes a black eye and bruised ribs (C shows them). Beaten, Lefty gives up the Swede; beating Danny, he gloats, and the job stays open - talk is still there, a threat is harder, and he will fight again on another night. The Doc can patch up the bruises.
- **Telling Rourke.** Back at his table: "About Lindqvist's, Mr. Rourke..." Still working on it; the truth (and Lefty's twenty handed over, or kept quiet about); or "It's handled" when it is not. Rourke takes a deal with a shrug (satisfied), a man leaned on or beaten with a grin (pleased), a killing uneasily ("a dead North Side man brings more North Side men"); he never turns on Danny for using his own judgement. His reward ($10, $5 for a killing) comes out of his purse - what Danny has handed him - then off what Danny owes him, and the rest out of the next envelope. Danny can also give the Swede his money back (Lindqvist does not forget it, and neither does Rourke).
- **Talk.** Lefty brags to Eddie, Eddie tells Augie, Augie tells Rourke: three nights later Rourke knows about Lefty's twenty, and the Swede complains about still paying if Danny said it was handled when it was not. A lie found out costs trust, patience and the money (and after "it's handled", Rourke still wants the job done; Danny can still settle it before the Swede talks). The ward talks about how it was settled ("Word is you beat Lefty Moran with your bare hands").

## Dangerous company (Milestone 006)

- **Voices.** The first time Danny talks to Augie, to Rourke (who has not seen him in two or three years) and to Eddie, they greet him in their own recorded voices; Eddie has another line the first time Danny asks to see his wares ("Let's see what you've got"). The greeting is the page of the talk; the usual business follows. One voice at a time; the night steps back while someone speaks; walking away ends it. The first meetings are remembered in the saved game.
- **The Bird** sings "Leave the Lights Low" at a ribbon microphone at the front of the Copper Owl's bandstand, in her own spotlight (emerald gown, white feather boa, auburn waves), swaying to the slow ballad. Walk up to her (E, "Interrupt the Bird"): the needle scratches off the record, the song stops and she has words for him; the first time, "Do I know you?" gets her introduction, after the complaint. Ask her for secrets (what the club has heard) or about the song. When Danny walks away the band counts her back in and the song starts from the top.
- **Lefty Moran** (camel overcoat, white fedora, a cigar), a North Side gangster who does not work for Rourke, loiters on the sidewalk outside Doyle's Garage on Pruitt Street. He will exchange a word. Shoot at him - hit him, or put a round past his ear - and he draws: he keeps his distance and fires every second or two while he can see Danny, follows where he lost him, cools off after a while (but not if he sees Danny close by again that night), and runs when badly hurt. Shot dead, he stays dead.
- **Wounds.** A round lands in the head, the body, an arm or a leg (the side it came from decides which). Grazed, wounded, badly hurt: wounded legs make a person limp, a wounded gun arm spoils the aim; a bad head wound, two in the body or enough in all brings a person down - they fall, turned on their side, darker, a dark patch beneath them. Passers-by, club patrons, the beat cop and Lefty can die (the next evening the streets have other passers-by, the club its patrons and the beat a new man). The ward's own people - Augie, Rourke, the Bird, Sal, Novak, Lindqvist, Eddie, Doc Pettibone - go down badly hurt and are carried off to the Doc's for the rest of the night; they are back the next evening and they remember (so does the ward, and Rourke, who takes it worst if it was him or one of his own at the club). Danny never dies: going down he comes to on the Doc's table an hour later, patched up in a hurry, the Doc's fee taken. C shows where he is hurt.
- **Pettibone Animal Hospital** (Pruitt Street, next to the warehouse; a paw-print badge): Doc Pettibone treats dogs, cats, the odd dray horse and, for cash and no questions, the ward's gangsters. A tiled surgery with an examining table under a cold lamp, a cabinet of brown bottles, a basset hound asleep in a kennel and a bench for the waiting room. "Patch me up, Doc": a $2 visit plus $1.50 a graze, $4 a wound, $8 for anything badly hurt.
- **The Morgue**: the long greystone at the east end of Pruitt Street, its name in pale letters on the roof and one dim lamp over its doors. A landmark (with a slate-grey badge on the ward map); there is nothing to do there.
- **Cars.** Danny's own sedan is parked outside his flat ("Get in your Sedan"). Every other parked car belongs to somebody ("Take the Roadster (not yours)"): taking it is theft - once per car per night - and if the beat cop is close enough to see it, with no building in the way, he comes to look and gives chase when he sees the thief. By the next evening the cars are back with their owners.
- **The ward map** (M): the whole ward, its streets and landmarks, the places with their badges, Danny, his car and the beat cop.
- **U-turns.** At a crawl the cars have a little more lock: they turn round on Marlowe Avenue (and at the crossings); in the side streets it takes a three-point turn. At driving pace they steer exactly as before.
- **Headlights**: two beams, one from each lamp, running together into one pool further down the road.

## The ward's business (Milestone 005)

Every place has a till, stock, customers, bills and a supplier, and the numbers carry on whether Danny is there or not (`src/economy/neighbourhood.gd`; provisional, in cents and units):

| Place | Who | Sells | Trades | Supplier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Copper Owl | Augie | drinks (from a cellar of 14 bottles) and the cover charge | through the night, as the clock runs | none since Augie's man got pinched: its cellar fills only with the rye Danny brings |
| Sal's Barber Shop | Sal | haircuts, and rye from his cupboard (10) | by day | his Cicero man, 6 bottles a morning |
| Novak's Grocery | Mr. Novak | groceries (shelves of 12 crates) | by day | the truck, 10 crates each morning, cash on delivery |
| Eddie's back room | Eddie | boxes of .45 rounds (crate of 6) | when Danny buys | one box a day |

Each morning the supplier comes and is paid from the till; a till emptied in the night (protection, a bad day) means a short delivery, empty shelves and thin takings the next day — and a short delivery at a business Rourke protects is talked about. Rent and wages are owed even when the till is empty. Nothing is random: the same nights give the same ward.

**Rourke** sits at his corner table in the Copper Owl. The protection money (Augie's, Novak's) is his; Danny keeps a commission (normally a fifth) and his rye trade is his own. At dusk Rourke works out what he expects: what he believes each business can pay (good, short or broke, from what he sees of the club and what he has heard), at least most of what Danny brought lately, what is still owed, and a tenth of any rye he watched Danny sell over Augie's bar. He never knows what Danny actually took: Augie tells him at once when he has paid; Novak's payment reaches him as talk two nights later — and if it shows Danny lied about a thin night, he finds out then. Trust and patience follow how Danny settles up. Out of patience, Rourke cuts Danny's commission, then tells Augie not to buy his rye until they are square, and after another night his boys are waiting on the stairs in the morning to take what is owed.

**People remember** (`src/economy/memories.gd`): threats, favours, deals, disputes, lies and broken promises, fading over the nights. Novak warms to a man who lent him money for the truck and keeps his distance from one who leaned on him; Augie remembers being leaned on; Sal knocks a dime off for a regular. **Talk spreads** (`src/economy/rumours.gd`) one person a night along who talks to whom: Novak to Sal, Augie and Lindqvist; Sal to Novak and Augie; Augie to Sal, Rourke, Eddie and Novak; Rourke only to Augie; Lindqvist to Novak and Eddie; Eddie to Lindqvist and Augie. Ask Sal, Augie or Rourke what they have heard.

**The incident:** when a shopkeeper's till cannot cover his delivery (Novak, most often, after paying protection), the truck brings less, the shelves empty and the word goes round. Danny can ignore it, lean on him anyway, or lend him the money for the truck (repaid with a quarter on top within three nights, and remembered).

- The Copper Owl's singer is heard through the club's walls along Marlowe Avenue: muffled and low (the bass carries), louder and a little brighter at the door, from the club's side of you; inside the club it opens up into the full recording with the room's chatter, glasses and footsteps under it. From the next room (your flat) it is fainter still; from a car it sinks under the engine within a block or so. It never stops or restarts: walk in and out as often as you like.

## The house always wins (Milestone 013)

- **Leo's wheel.** The wheel runs all night while Danny is in the gaming room: Leo opens the bets ("place your bets") with the head turning, sends the ball round with the bets still open, calls "last bets" and "no more bets", the ball drops into the number drawn at the call, he marks it and pays, pauses, and opens the next. Danny steps up whenever he likes - to bet while the bets are open, or only to watch; after the call he waits for the next round. A brass ring round the bowl runs down to the call and holds a moment (2.5 s, at most 6 s past its time) while Danny is still putting chips down. Watching from the room, Leo's calls are over his head.
- **The regulars** (fictional): Mrs. Edna Halloway (cautious - red, black, the dozens), Big Stan (big chips on columns and dozens, a quarter on seventeen), Bertie Sloane (his own numbers, with a little notebook system), Dolly Crane (the Owl) and Mick Tierney (the Star). Each comes in through the evening by the card room's door, keeps to his own places (the rail, a machine, a drink at a little table, a corner to watch the wheel), and goes home by four. At the wheel they play their own colour chips (never Danny's money, never the draw); at the machines their pulls are heard where they stand; they take wins and losses in their own words, and now and then two of them pass the time. A regular at a machine Danny walks up to gives it up; the Bell is always free.
- **The machines.** Stakes of one, two or five coins (S); the plate prints what each line pays at the stake. A pull that pays nothing may light the HOLD lamps (the Owl and the Star one losing pull in three, the Bell one in four); hold one or two reels (1-3 or a click on the reel or its brass latch) and pull again for the same stake: the held reels stand still, the others spin, all three are paid; then the holds come off and the next pull spins all three. Plain pulls return about 85%, the best possible hold play about 94-95%.
- **The card table.** The stakes chosen on the buy-in (nickel and dime as ever; dime and quarter; quarter and half), with buy-ins and the regulars' stacks to match; a plaque at the table says which. Getting up during a hand Danny is still in asks first. Chips slide into the pot and to the winner; a copper marker shows who is to act.
- **The room's air.** Smoke drifts under the ceiling; the lamps are never quite steady; the murmur of two regulars talking, glasses, chips, a machine's lever across the room.
- **Cars and saving.** Cars stay where they are left, across saves and districts; a game saved at the wheel loads at the wheel.
